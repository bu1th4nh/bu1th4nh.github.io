01DataSection and spot

Two units: the section, and the spot inside it.

Every modality count in this deck is per spot, within one section.

Section (slice)

One thin slice cut from a tissue block and placed on one slide. A dataset is one section or a series of them; "slice" means the same.

Spot (pixel)

One capture site on that section: 100 µm in ST, 55 µm in Visium, 20 or 10 µm in DBiT-based assays. One spot is one row of every count matrix.

Modalities per spot

What one spot of one section reads: 1 in ST; 2 in most of our data (RNA + protein, or RNA + ATAC); 3 in spatial tri-omics; at most 5, in Spatial-Mux-seq.

Across sections

The P22 brain has ATAC and three histone marks, but each section pairs only one of them with RNA: 2 per spot, never 5. Joining sections that share no spot is the mosaic problem.

Read the next slides asmodalities in one spot of one section. Counts across a series of sections are not added up.

Clicks: 1: Cut a thin slice from the tissue block, and lay it on a glass slide: a section. 2: The assay tiles the section with spots, or pixels, 10 to 100 µm wide. 3: Every molecule read in a spot carries that spot's barcode, whatever its modality. 4: Modalities per spot = what one spot reads. Two sections never share a spot. The barcode is why paired assays are paired: RNA, antibody tags and ATAC fragments from one spot carry the same spatial barcode, so they land in the same row. Different sections, even adjacent ones, have different spots, so their modalities must be aligned before they can be compared. (≈2:00)

03Recipe 1 of 745 of 60 papers · 32 from 2026

Spatial domains: cluster, map back, score.

The most common experiment. Cluster the joint embedding, colour the tissue by cluster, then score the map with labels and without them.

Recipe

  1. Cluster. mclust on the joint embedding (SpatialGlue, MultiSP) or Leiden (COSMOS). MultiSP sets K to the number of annotated regions; SpatialGlue chose K by hand.

  2. Map back. Colour each spot by its cluster at its (x, y).

  3. Score with labels. ARI and NMI against the manual annotation; report the mean over seeds.

  4. Score without labels. Moran's I of each cluster's map (SpatialGlue, via Squidpy): do neighbouring spots agree?

Worked example
COSMOS on P22: ARI 0.63, against 0.43 for SpatialGlue (Fig. 3c). SpatialGlue on lymph node A1: median Moran's I 0.62, with 6 clusters against 10 annotated categories (Fig. 2e, g).

Pitfall
The annotation is one view of the tissue: in lymph node A1 the paracortex "more resembled the RNA-specific and protein-specific modalities than the H&E annotation" (SpatialGlue).

Long et al., Nat. Methods 2024 (SpatialGlue), Fig. 2e, g, Results and Supplementary Information · Zhou et al., Nat. Commun. 2025 (COSMOS), Fig. 3c · Mo C, Zou X, Jin S. Cell Genomics 6, 101141 (2026) (MultiSP), doi:10.1016/j.xgen.2026.101141 · Hubert & Arabie, J. Classif. 1985 (ARI) · Moran, Biometrika 1950 · Scrucca et al., R J. 2016 (mclust).

Step 1: the embedding is clustered; ellipses show the K groups. Step 2: the same labels painted on the tissue, as a wave. Step 3: outlines are the manual annotation, red rings where the two disagree; ARI and NMI count that agreement. Step 4: without labels, check spatial coherence: the lens shows one spot and its six neighbours. Note the K choice: MultiSP uses the annotated count, SpatialGlue tuned K by eye, 6 clusters on a 10-category annotation. The pitfall line is SpatialGlue's own: the H&E annotation can disagree with the molecules. (≈1:45)

03Recipe 2 of 719 of 60 papers · 14 from 2026

Modality weights: which layer led each spot.

Read the per-spot weights the model learned, map them, average them per domain, and test what they claim.

Recipe

  1. Extract. Per-spot weights: SpatialGlue's between-modality attention is a softmax, so the weights sum to 1; COSMOS uses WNN weights.

  2. Map. Colour each spot by its RNA weight against the other modality's.

  3. Summarise. Per domain, as split violins (SpatialGlue Fig. 3e) or mean bars; a domain led by one modality is a claim to check.

  4. Check. Rerun without that modality (MISO reruns every combination), or map private factors (SpaMV).

Worked example
SpatialGlue, P22 ATAC–RNA: RNA weighs more in the corpus callosum, ATAC in cortex and lateral ventricle (Fig. 3e). SpaMV, E13 embryo: the H3K27ac-private topic sits in the spinal cord, the H3K27me3-private topic in jaw and nose (Fig. 4e).

Pitfall
SpatialGlue's weights "are calculated based on the latent embeddings of each modality, rather than raw feature matrices", so some discrepancy with the raw data is expected. MISO "cannot directly quantify the contribution of each modality".

Long et al., Nat. Methods 2024 (SpatialGlue), Fig. 3e and Results · Zhou et al., Nat. Commun. 2025 (COSMOS), Fig. 3d · Liu et al., Nat. Commun. 2026 (SpaMV), Fig. 4e · Coleman et al., Nat. Methods 2025 (MISO), Discussion, Extended Data Figs 8–9.

One spot first: the attention layer turns two embeddings into two weights that sum to 1 (the 0.7/0.3 is a made-up example). Step 2 paints those weights on the tissue: blue where RNA leads, orange where protein leads. Step 3 averages per domain. Step 4 is the check: a weight is only a claim until removing that modality changes the result, or until a private factor shows a region only one modality sees. Six of the eight catalogue papers that name modality weights or shared-versus-private factors are from 2026. (≈1:45)

03Recipe 3 of 733 of 60 papers · 22 from 2026

Domain markers: test, rank, map back.

How a domain map gets biological names. Run it for every modality: genes, peaks, proteins.

Recipe

  1. Contrast. One domain against all the others.

  2. Test every feature. Wilcoxon rank-sum: Seurat FindAllMarkers for genes, ArchR for peaks (SpatialGlue); adjust for multiple testing.

  3. Rank. Top markers per domain by fold change, shown as a dot plot or heat map.

  4. Map back. The top marker should outline its domain and match known anatomy.

Worked example
SpatialGlue, P22 H3K27ac–RNA: Tspan2, Cldn11 and Ugt8a mark the corpus callosum and anterior commissure; Olfm1, Cux2 and Rorb mark cortical layers (Fig. 3j).

Pitfall
Double dipping: when the groups come from clustering the same data, a classical test "yields an extremely inflated type I error rate" (Gao et al. 2024); use p-values to rank, not to prove. Test every modality: in SpaMV's thymus, RNA-only markers would mislabel a protein-defined cluster as T-cell-enriched (Supp. Fig. 19).

Long et al., Nat. Methods 2024 (SpatialGlue), Fig. 3j and Supplementary Information · Liu et al., Nat. Commun. 2026 (SpaMV), Supp. Fig. 19 · Gao LL, Bien J, Witten D. Selective inference for hierarchical clustering. J. Am. Stat. Assoc. 119, 332–342 (2024), doi:10.1080/01621459.2022.2116331, abstract.

Run in 33 of the 60 papers; this is the step that names the domains. One domain against the rest; each point on the volcano is one feature; the dot plot shows the top markers per domain; step 4 paints the top marker back on the tissue. The pitfall is statistical: the domains were found in the same data, so p-values are optimistic (Gao, Bien and Witten 2024; count splitting by Neufeld et al. 2024 is one fix). The SpaMV thymus example shows why markers must be tested in every modality. (≈1:45)

03Recipe 4 of 713 of 60 papers · 10 from 2026

Cross-modal imputation: hide, predict, compare.

Hold out a modality where it was measured, predict it from the rest, and score the prediction against the measurement.

Recipe

  1. Hold out. Remove protein or ATAC from one section and train on the others; SpaMosaic uses each of 3 sections as the test set once.

  2. Predict. From RNA, through nearest neighbours in the shared latent space (SpaMosaic) or a regressor on an RNA-only embedding (MultiSpaceNet).

  3. Compare. Pearson correlation per feature and per spot; AUROC on binarised peaks for ATAC (SpaMosaic).

  4. Baseline. Beat a simple predictor on the same split, such as a Ridge regression on raw RNA.

Worked example
MultiSpaceNet, spleen replicate 2, five-fold cross-validation: mean Pearson r 0.651, against 0.547 for SpatialGlue and 0.582 for a Ridge regression on raw RNA (Table 3).

Pitfall
Leakage: an embedding fused with protein has "already consumed the protein it would be asked to predict" (MultiSpaceNet). The ground truth matters too: smoothing sparse ATAC raised SpaMosaic's spot-level PCC by 34.4% (preprint Supp. Fig. S34b).

Zhang Z et al. Bioinformatics 42, btag676 (2026) (MultiSpaceNet), doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btag676, Table 3 · Yan et al., Nat. Genet. 2026 (SpaMosaic), preprint doi:10.1101/2024.10.02.616189 v3, Supplementary Information and Supp. Fig. S34.

Step 1 empties the protein map: that section's protein is held out. Step 2 fills it from RNA. Step 3 is the check, one protein at a time, measured against predicted. Step 4: a simple baseline on the same split; MultiSpaceNet shows a plain Ridge regression on raw RNA beats SpatialGlue's embedding on spleen. Leakage is the trap: if the embedding was trained with the protein, it has seen the answer. MultiSpaceNet reports the leaky and clean protocols differ by at most 0.016 on its data, so check rather than assume. (≈1:45)

03Recipe 5 of 721 of 60 papers · 16 from 2026

Multi-section: mix the sections, keep the domains.

Score two axes at once: are the sections mixed, and are the domains still apart? Then check that matching regions line up.

Recipe

  1. Embed. Integrate every section into one embedding.

  2. Mixing. iLISI, kBET or batch ASW, with points labelled by section.

  3. Biology kept. ARI, NMI or cLISI, with points labelled by domain; scIB-style scores weight the two 40/60 (PRESENT).

  4. Align. Check that matching regions line up across sections, by registration or optimal transport.

Worked example
SpaMosaic, embryonic brain, 3 sections: MIDAS ranked first on iLISI and SpaMosaic fourth (preprint Fig. 3g), yet MIDAS's clusters "showed substantial overlapping".

Pitfall
Mixing alone is not success. Scales differ too: SMART normalises iLISI to 0–1 on graph distances, so values do not compare across papers; and single-section ARI "does not, by itself, test integration" (MultiSpaceNet).

Yan et al., Nat. Genet. 2026 (SpaMosaic), preprint v3 Fig. 3g · Li Z et al. Brief. Bioinform. 27, bbag214 (2026) (PRESENT), doi:10.1093/bib/bbag214 · Du Z et al., Nat. Commun. 17, 2876 (2026) (SMART) · Zhang et al. 2026 (MultiSpaceNet) · Korsunsky et al., Nat. Methods 2019 (LISI) · Büttner et al., Nat. Methods 2019 (kBET) · Luecken et al., Nat. Methods 2022 (scIB).

Circles are section A, squares section B. Before integration they sit apart by section. Step 1 moves them together. Step 2 recolours by domain: the domains must still be separate, otherwise the mixing erased biology. Step 3 names the two scores; PRESENT and other scIB-style papers weight batch removal 40 and biology 60. Step 4 aligns the sections themselves. The SpaMosaic example is the trade-off in one line: the best mixer blurred the clusters. (≈1:45)

03Recipe 6 of 78 of 60 papers · 5 from 2026

Trajectories: order spots in time and space.

Pseudotime orders spots within one section; optimal transport links sections from successive stages.

Recipe

  1. Root. Pick the start from biology: "the first cell of a given cell type" (COSMOS), or progenitor states (CoMo).

  2. Pseudotime. Diffusion pseudotime (scanpy DPT) on the joint embedding (COSMOS, CoMo), or PAGA (SpatialCOC); map it on the tissue.

  3. Check. Genes correlated with pseudotime should be known markers; COSMOS found 577 on P22 (Fig. 3g–i).

  4. Across stages. Couple spots of successive time points by optimal transport on expression and spatial structure (moscot).

Worked example
CoMo, E13 embryo ATAC–RNA: pseudotime runs from the ventricular zone to subpallium to pallium (Fig. 4e). moscot, mouse embryo E9.5–E16.5: annotation transitions 5% and 13% more accurate than moscot.time and TOME (Fig. 4b).

Pitfall
On one section, pseudotime is a spatial gradient, not elapsed time, and neither COSMOS nor CoMo scores it with a number. moscot's couplings "probabilistically link cells": they are model output, not lineage.

03Recipe 7 of 716 of 60 papers · 11 from 2026

Regulatory links: peaks to genes, motifs to domains.

Needs chromatin and RNA on the same spots, or an imputed pair. Correlate, compare with a matched null, then validate outside the data.

Recipe

  1. Link. Correlate each peak's accessibility with a nearby gene's expression across spots (Signac LinkPeaks in SWITCH), or learn the links by attention within 150 kb (MultiGATE).

  2. Null. Compare each correlation with the one expected for peaks of the same GC content, accessibility and length (Signac).

  3. Motifs. TF motif deviations per spot with chromVAR (SWITCH), averaged per domain.

  4. Validate. Against outside data: eQTL, enhancer atlases, ChIP-seq (MultiGATE).

Worked example
MultiGATE, human hippocampus ATAC–RNA: its peak–gene links recover GTEx eQTL with AUROC 0.703, against 0.530 for Cicero and 0.515 for Spearman correlation (Fig. 2d).

Pitfall
Two features that both mark one domain correlate with no regulatory link between them. SWITCH's authors concede motif plus correlation "may not allow unambiguous assignment of regulatory roles" (Peer Review File), and its links use imputed ATAC.

Miao J et al., Nat. Commun. 16, 9403 (2025) (MultiGATE), doi:10.1038/s41467-025-63418-x, Fig. 2d, Methods · Li et al., Nat. Comput. Sci. 2025 (SWITCH), Supplementary Information and Peer Review File · Stuart et al., Nat. Methods 2021 (Signac; LinkPeaks documentation) · Schep et al., Nat. Methods 2017 (chromVAR).

The track is one gene with accessible peaks nearby. Step 1 draws a link for each peak, thicker for a higher correlation; grey ones fail. Step 2 is why the null matters: Signac compares every correlation with background peaks of the same GC content, accessibility and length. Step 3 moves from single peaks to TF motifs per spot. Step 4: only outside data can confirm a link; MultiGATE uses GTEx eQTL. The pitfall is my reading, not a paper's: domain structure alone makes markers correlate. This is the analysis our triomics LPC data would need. (≈1:45)

Applications check the platforms, then read the biology.

Four 2025–26 studies where the experiments are the point: cross-platform checks, same-section metabolites, a negative control.

07ReviewA reviewer's checklist (our inference)

What to ask of an integration paper.

Our checklist, drawn from the corpus: each item names a paper that already does it.

  • Domains: K and how it was chosen; spread over seeds (SCIGMA); one label-free score (smiMAD).
  • Baselines: a naive concatenation and an untrained network (MultiSpaceNet).
  • Weights: corrupt or remove a modality and see whether they follow (MultiSpaceNet, MISO).
  • Imputation: held-out data that never enters the model; a leakage audit (MultiSpaceNet).
  • Multi-section: mixing and conservation together; a wrong-alignment control (MIIT).
  • Regulation: links validated outside the data: eQTL, Hi-C, ChIP (MultiGATE, ISON).
  • Uncertainty: error against uncertainty, not just a map (no paper read does this).
  • Data: at least one section beyond lymph node, P22, embryo and spleen.
